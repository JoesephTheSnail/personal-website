The average person gets dozens of push notifications a day. Each one is a small interruption, and the focus it breaks takes longer to recover than the notification took to read. Most people have accepted that constant distraction as normal.

## The pitch

This pitch proposed an app that batches incoming notifications by priority and delivers them in short, scheduled digests instead of one at a time. Users choose when and how often they want updates. The app:

- Summarizes what happened
- Surfaces what's actually urgent
- Filters out the noise

## The core insight

Notifications are designed to pull your attention toward the app that sent them, not to be useful to you. A summarizer flips that: you decide when to engage, not the apps.

Presented at DECA Provincials.

**What I took from it:**
- Notifications are built for the app's engagement, not your benefit. That matters more than the sheer volume.
- The best ideas solve problems people have stopped questioning. Constant interruption feels normal now, which is why there's room to fix it.
- Competition pressure forces clarity. When you explain your thinking to judges in two minutes, every weak assumption shows fast.
