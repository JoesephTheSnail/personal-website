The prompt was to find something interesting in carbon capture and synthetic biology. We knew it before the hackathon started, which gave us a head start.

## The idea

We landed on genetically modifying banana plants so they absorb more CO₂ than they naturally do. Bananas grow fast, grow in the tropics, and are farmed worldwide at huge scale. That makes them a plausible way to spread extra carbon uptake, if the biology can be made to work.

Our core argument was a banana plant that fixes more carbon per hectare than current varieties, grown on existing farmland without displacing food. Whether that's technically feasible was the open question, and that's where we wanted the conversation to go.

## How we worked

- **Roles first.** The night before, we mapped each person's skills and assigned roles. When the clock started, we were already doing the work.
- **Time-boxed research.** Set a timer, go wide, stop. Whatever we found was our working set. The limit made us more decisive.
- **About an hour on the deck.** We balanced visual design with the research, and time-boxed each phase.

---

**Outcome:** Developed and presented a synthetic biology carbon capture concept: engineered banana plants as a scalable CO₂ sink deployed through existing agriculture.

**What I took from it:**
- Assign roles before you start, not during. It's one of the simplest habits that saves the most time.
- Time-boxing gives cleaner results than open-ended research. The limit makes you use what you have instead of chasing the perfect source.
- Lean into what's uncertain. Feasibility was our open question, and saying so made the pitch more credible, not less.
