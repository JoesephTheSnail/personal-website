## The problem

Most mental health apps are too clinical, too expensive, or too adult-coded to reach teenagers. The ones that do reach teens often feel patronizing or gamified in ways that hurt the content.

## The approach

I designed it to treat users like adults while keeping the experience light and non-intimidating. The app includes:

- Mood tracking
- CBT-based journaling prompts
- Breathing exercises
- A resource library

It's modular, so people can use only the pieces that feel useful to them.

## What I built

The whole product was built in Figma: a full high-fidelity prototype with interactive flows, a component library, and a design system. Every screen was designed with accessibility in mind: contrast ratios, tap targets, and readable type at small sizes.

This project pushed my product thinking more than any other. When your user is a stressed 16-year-old at 2 a.m., every friction point matters. I went through five major redesigns before landing on something that served the user, not just my portfolio.

---

**Outcome:** Built a full high-fidelity Figma prototype, with interactive flows, component library, and design system, for a modular teen mental wellness app after five major redesigns.

**What I took from it:**
- Most apps fail teenagers because the design talks down to them, not because the content is wrong. Respecting your user's intelligence is a design decision.
- A stressed 16-year-old at 2 a.m. changes what "usable" means. Every friction point matters more.
- The first version that looks good isn't the first version that works. Being willing to throw it out is what separates product thinking from taste.
