## The problem

Insulin-dependent diabetes means constant management. Every meal, workout, and stressful day needs a manual decision: how much insulin, when, and how. Mistakes have serious consequences, and the load on patients is huge.

InsuSense started with one question: what if the system could close the loop?

## The prototype

It connects to continuous glucose monitors (CGMs), reads real-time glucose data, and triggers insulin delivery automatically. That's a step toward a closed-loop artificial pancreas.

The hardest engineering challenge was building a delivery mechanism that's reliable, miniaturized, and safe enough to trust with medication.

## What it took

I had to learn the biology and the engineering at the same time. Before I could design anything that wouldn't over- or under-dose, I had to understand how glucose and insulin behave: lag times, correction factors, and carb ratios.

The research was one of the most intensive I've done. The rules for any device that delivers medication are strict for good reason. It gave me a lot of respect for the people who build medical devices professionally.

---

**Outcome:** Designed a non-invasive insulin delivery prototype that reads real-time glucose data from a CGM and automates dosing, a step toward a closed-loop artificial pancreas.

**What I took from it:**
- Closed-loop medical systems need the biology and the engineering together. Lag times, correction factors, and dosing logic are all connected.
- Clinical rules exist for good reason. Designing around them early separates a concept that could ship from one that can't.
- This was my most intensive research, and it gave me the most respect for healthcare engineering. The stakes make every design decision heavier.
