I almost left at 6 p.m. I had a long day ahead the next morning and planned to put in a few hours and call it.

I didn't leave. I got so absorbed that I stayed well past midnight. My teammates Bianca and Shreya stayed even later, until 4 a.m.

## The problem

Rising CO₂ is making the ocean more acidic.

- Global ocean pH fell from 8.11 in 1985 to 8.05 in 2021.
- In Alaska, where colder water absorbs more CO₂, it's already 8.01.
- Acidic water damages the shells of oysters, clams and other marine life. Alaska supplies over 60% of US seafood, so the effects on human health are real.

## Our solution

We proposed building enclosed ocean environments where phytoplankton can thrive. Phytoplankton absorb CO₂ through photosynthesis at huge scale. In an enclosure, the uptake can be managed, measured and improved, without releasing genetically modified organisms into open water.

## Two things I still use

- **Time management under real pressure.** With 24 hours and a real problem, you triage constantly. It's different from time management in theory.
- **Root cause analysis.** Going one level deeper than the surface problem almost always shows you a better place to act.

---

**Outcome:** Stayed through the night to build a full research-backed pitch: phytoplankton enclosures as a managed CO₂ sink, delivered as a complete deck and memo by morning.

**What I took from it:**
- Real pressure shows how you actually work. I had every reason to leave at 6 p.m. and didn't, which told me what absorbs me.
- Root cause analysis is more than a framework. When time is short, going one level deeper stops you from building a great solution to the wrong problem.
- Time management at midnight with a real deadline is a different skill from the theory. You only build it by being in it.
