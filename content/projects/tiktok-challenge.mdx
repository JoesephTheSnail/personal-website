This was a 20-day TKS challenge. My team was Ryan Kadiri, Sara Mohanty, and Jonathan Ye. Ryan and I handled outreach, and Sara and Jonathan did the research.

## How it ran

The first half was outreach and discovery. The second half was synthesis and building the deck.

## Outreach was the hard part

I was traveling for over a week of our research window. That left me three days to get enough creator responses. I messaged as many creators as I could, including Mark Rober, just in case. He did not reply.

I stayed in the loop through the meetings I could make, and the project kept moving.

## Two perspectives

We deliberately looked at both creators and consumers.

- **Creators** showed us what the platform makes easy and what frustrates them.
- **Consumers** told us what viewers actually want.

Holding both at once made our recommendations useful instead of one-sided.

## The last week

We built the deck. The last day was stressful, but we pulled it together. The biggest lesson: gather enough, not everything. Limited time forces you to work with what you have, and that often gives cleaner thinking than unlimited information.

---

**Outcome:** Completed a 20-day sprint researching the TikTok creator experience through outreach, user interviews, and dual-perspective analysis, delivered as a full pitch deck.

**What I took from it:**
- Gather enough, not everything. A hard deadline forces you to synthesize instead of pile up.
- Research from two sides. Creators and consumers together made our recommendations useful. One side alone gives you half the picture, usually the half that agrees with you.
- Staying in the loop while travelling is a skill. I missed meetings and still kept things moving. Momentum matters more than presence.
