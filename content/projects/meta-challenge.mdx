This was a TKS case study challenge, and our team was given a problem straight from Meta:

*"What can Meta do to ensure the safety of young people that use its products? As Meta ventures more into the Metaverse, what ideas are important to consider to provide a high quality, non-harmful experience for younger platform users?"*

## Two things at once

We had to think about the existing platforms (Instagram, Facebook) and the emerging Metaverse, where the risks are different.

- On a screen, you can regulate content.
- In an immersive 3D space, harm is spatial and harder to filter.

## Our approach

Our approach centered on three areas:

1. **Age verification** that actually works.
2. **Behavioral monitoring** inside immersive environments.
3. **Parental oversight tools** that don't kill the user experience.

We backed each proposal with our research survey, which gathered real views from young users and parents.

## What stood out

Safety and engagement aren't opposites. The Metaverse only succeeds if users, especially younger ones, feel safe enough to stay. Meta's business interest and safety are more aligned than the public debate suggests.

---

**Outcome:** Developed safety recommendations for a challenge set directly by Meta, as part of a TKS case competition. They covered age verification, monitoring in immersive environments, and parental oversight tools, and were backed by original user research.

**What I took from it:**
- Safety and engagement aren't opposites. Platforms that feel unsafe lose users, so Meta's interest and safety point the same way.
- Immersive 3D spaces create different risks than screens. You can filter a piece of content. You can't easily filter an experience, because the harm happens in the moment.
- A real survey changed the quality of our recommendations. User research surfaces things you'd never find by thinking about the problem from outside.
