## The idea

What if someone who can't get therapy, because of cost, availability, or stigma, could still get real psychoeducation and evidence-based coping tools through a conversation?

The goal is to fill the gap for the majority of people who will never see a therapist, not to replace therapists.

## What it does

Built with the Gemini API, the chatbot focuses on psychoeducation, not diagnosis or treatment. It can:

- Walk users through grounding techniques
- Explain cognitive distortions in plain language
- Help someone find their stress triggers through guided conversation
- Point toward appropriate resources when the situation calls for it

## The hardest part: safety

Any AI in a mental health setting has to be careful about what it says and what it doesn't. I spent a lot of time on guardrails and edge cases, especially crisis scenarios, before the core features.

The project deepened my interest in AI and healthcare, and directly shaped the MindSet app design that followed.

---

**Outcome:** Built a working AI mental health chatbot using the Gemini API. It's designed for psychoeducation and evidence-based coping tools, with safety guardrails for crisis scenarios.

**What I took from it:**
- In sensitive areas, safety is the foundation, not a feature added at the end. I spent more time on guardrails than on core features.
- The mental health gap is about access more than quality of care. Most people who need support will never see a therapist, and that's the problem worth solving.
- Framing matters. Psychoeducation feels different to a user than diagnosis, even when the information is the same.
