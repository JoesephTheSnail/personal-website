In Grade 9 I drew a unit that bolts onto a diesel truck and catches CO₂ before it leaves the tailpipe. I never checked whether it would actually help. Click <a href="https://medium.com/@arnav0/eco-trucks-3c9d5dc10f63" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">here</a> to read the original Grade 9 version.

So I went back and worked it out properly.

## How it works

The key is a liquid called amine. It grabs CO₂ like a sponge soaks up water, but only when it's cool.

1. **Cool it.** Some of the exhaust is piped in and cooled to about 40°C.
2. **Soak it up.** A spray of amine soaks up the CO₂. The cleaner gas goes on its way.
3. **Squeeze it out.** Heating the amine to 90 to 100°C makes it let go of the CO₂.
4. **Store and reuse.** The CO₂ goes into tanks on the truck. The amine goes back for more.

The heat comes from the exhaust itself, so there's no separate burner. A small bin of spare amine tops things up as the liquid wears out.

## Current limitations

Running the system takes power, and that power costs CO₂ too. So how much it catches isn't the real number. What's left after you take that away is.

That's why I added a unit that turns leftover exhaust heat back into electricity.

## Why it matters

Diesel trucks are some of the hardest vehicles to switch to electric, and they put out CO₂ the whole time they drive.

Catching it at the tailpipe could cut emissions from trucks that will be on the road for a long time yet. But only if the system removes more than it costs to run.

**Outcome:** Turned a Grade 9 idea into a worked-out system for capturing CO₂ from truck exhaust, plus a 3D model that shows how each part works.

**What I took from it:**
- Captured isn't the same as saved. What's left after the system's own energy use is the number that counts, and it's a lot smaller.
- Going back to an old idea means finding out what you got wrong. Most of my fixes were to assumptions, not the drawing.
- If you can't explain something, build it. The 3D model showed me parts of my layout that didn't fit together.
