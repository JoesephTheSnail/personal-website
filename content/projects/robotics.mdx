I started getting into robotics in early 2026, and it's been one of the most humbling things I've worked on.

Software is forgiving. You can iterate fast, errors are reversible, and feedback is quick. Hardware is the opposite. A robot doesn't care about your logic if the motors can't carry it out.

## Where I'm at

My early focus is on fundamentals: control systems, sensors, and what it takes to make a machine move through an environment reliably. I wrote about starting out in my March 2026 newsletter.

Robotics combines what I find most interesting: AI decision-making, physical systems, and real-world limits that make you think differently than pure software does.

## Where I'm going

The long-term goal is to understand embodied AI: systems that don't just process data but act in the physical world. The gap between a language model and a robot that can reliably finish a task in a messy environment is huge, and I want to understand why.

This is early-stage work. More to come.

---

**Outcome:** Began building and programming autonomous robots, focusing on control systems, sensors, and the fundamentals of making machines navigate physical environments reliably.

**What I took from it:**
- Hardware is humbling in a way software isn't. Errors can't be undone, feedback is slow, and the machine doesn't care how elegant your logic is.
- The gap between an AI deciding and a robot doing is much bigger than it looks. That gap is where the interesting work is.
- Physical limits force a different kind of thinking. In software you can abstract almost anything. In robotics, physics wins.
