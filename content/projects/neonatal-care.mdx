Hypothermia is one of the leading causes of death in premature infants, especially in low-income countries where incubators are hard to get. Most existing solutions are expensive, need electricity, or are single-use. That means ongoing cost and waste for under-resourced clinics.

## The goal

The goal was simple: build something that actually works, can be reused, and costs almost nothing. The project was a deep dive into neonatal thermoregulation and the design limits of healthcare in low-resource settings.

## The result

The result is a reusable heat pouch for premature newborns:

- **Unit cost: $2.21**, a fraction of existing alternatives
- Uses phase-change materials to hold stable warmth for several hours
- Needs no electricity to activate
- Can be sterilized and reused again and again

## The hardest part

It wasn't the engineering. It was distribution and adoption. A good product doesn't help if clinics can't get it, can't afford it, or don't trust it. That system-level thinking reshaped my whole approach to the design.
